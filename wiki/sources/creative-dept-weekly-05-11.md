## Camera Philosophy — Message vs. Response

### During Message: Less Is More

Full consensus reached. The principle, stated by multiple people and affirmed by Pastor Nate:

> "Don't show anything that reminds me I am not in the room with the preacher."

Location attendees eventually stop thinking of the preacher as being on a screen — they process it as if the speaker is in the room with them. Any shot other than a clean, consistent head-on shot interrupts that psychology and snaps them back to "oh, that's right, I'm not there." JC: "Less is more — do it really, really stinking well."

Speaker 4 (fresh eyes at LV): "I was shocked that it didn't feel as virtual. It felt like my pastor, and I am here at church."

### During Response: The Connection Opportunity

Zac made the case for multi-location connection shots during the response moment — not during message. He cited Elevation Greenville: at the close of the response, they showed all campus locations simultaneously. The effect was "we're doing this together — there are other people at other locations." Zac: "It felt like you were a part of something bigger."

Pastor Nate's nuance:
- This works *only* if the speaker knows it's coming and is intentional with it ("I see that hand in the balcony" — in LV, there is no balcony)
- Communication to all LPs in advance is essential: if Brian Tausch knows it's coming, he evaluates it differently
- The week ECC did connected response previously, JC called ahead, wrote it in the notes, communicated via help keys — it worked. The week it wasn't communicated, it didn't

JC: They did connected response previously; stopped because not all locations were musically strong enough to follow an MD feed. Now with M5s at all locations, the technical foundation has improved.

### Online vs. Location Broadcast — Two Different Audiences

JC and Matt raised that the YouTube/online feed of other churches is NOT what their location campuses receive. To truly evaluate the location experience, you'd have to sit in one of their campus locations. Watching Elevation's YouTube does not tell you how Elevation Greenville feels.

**Decision: Eagle Brook site visit.** JC and Matt plan to attend an Eagle Brook Saturday service at one of their campuses to experience a multisite broadcast as a location attendee. Evaluate: shots, response, feel.

## Production & Worship Playbook Refresh

JC made an explicit call: all production and worship handbooks need a full refresh. Framing:

> "We have new recruits and we have new people. You only have that grace period of ambiguity for a short amount of time. We get to build the culture for them."

The playbook refresh is about seizing the window while the team is in a reset season — before institutional drift sets in.

**Target deadline:** Team Emmanuel Conference — August 16, 2026. Matt: "Use June and July to build it. TEC is the moment to cast vision to volunteers and lay it out — you're with them centrally across all locations."

**What the playbook covers:**
- Camera shot lists (Camera 1 looks for this; Camera 2 looks for that)
- Photo/social checklists for event coverage
- Worship leader production language ("blue 42" = we're running live response to locations, band in key of X, listening to MD)
- Volunteer expectation standards from first moment on site

JC: "I learned that [the importance of the playbook] from this Sunday. I didn't have song lyrics and I didn't have people's order. That's not me — but that's like me not having a playbook that tells me what to expect."

## Website and Events Management

Same themes as director-sync-05-11, now with the full creative team:

**The problem:** Content lives in 5 different places (main events page, location pages, landing pages, pop-ups, CCB). Managing it is redundant and consumes time that should go to storytelling.

**The model:** App is the primary communication channel. Website events should be a single source that auto-populates. CCB events for locations auto-attach to the app's events tab. Nathan Grams confirmed this is working at SLP and he loves it — he inputs into CCB, it appears automatically.

**Rollout:** Events tab for locations rolling out now. Locations input their own events into CCB; it aggregates. Kylie will take over website event management. Tanner to train her on WordPress (no code required).

**Expiration:** WordPress events auto-remove once the date passes. The issue is content that's in multiple places — the event page removes it, but a landing page or pop-up still shows it. Fix: add an explicit "take it down" task to every event entry in ClickUp.

**Matt:** "We're operating on an old ship. We're patching holes. It's going to change — integrations, upgrade, the whole thing." Website overhaul vendor engagement already underway (see [[website-overhaul-04-29]]).
