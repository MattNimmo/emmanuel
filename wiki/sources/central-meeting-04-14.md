## EOS Introduction

Speaker 1 introduced EOS (Entrepreneurial Operating System) as a framework ECC is moving toward — "it could weave its way into some of our culture, our version of it, in three to six months."

### Core Concept: The Arrows Metaphor

> "There's a visual that shows a circle — the organization — with a bunch of arrows inside representing the people. Some arrows point north, some northeast, some south. Nobody's trying to do anything wrong. But when there are no organizational big goals, there's no mechanism to make sure everyone's aligned."

EOS addresses this by establishing:
1. **Org-wide annual goals** — singular and clear (example given: Mission 52)
2. **Quarterly rocks** — what are we doing in Q1, Q2, Q3, Q4 as a *church*, not as departments
3. **Departmental alignment** — how does kids align with the church quarterly rock? How does Lakeville align? How does Central align?

The result: "You go further faster because everybody's rowing the same direction."

### What This Means for Comms

- Comms will need to map its quarterly work to church-wide rocks, not just to its own project calendar
- This is one reason why Matt's capacity/priority framework matters — it positions comms to be a responsive partner to whatever the quarterly rock is, rather than a backlogged team that can't take on new direction
- See [[eos-framework]] for full concept detail

### The Comms Goal — Key Detail

Matt's stated SMART goal: **"Reduce or reprioritize work anytime we're above 85% capacity, so the team doesn't go beyond that threshold."**

This is the capacity planning framework in goal form. The 85% threshold is the trigger — not a ceiling to crash into, but a signal to reprioritize before quality or team health degrades. See [[communications-capacity-planning]].

## EOS Meeting Structure — Elements Introduced

Even before full EOS rollout, the facilitator used EOS-style meeting tools in this session:
- **Discussion/parking list:** A side list for topics that are starting to dominate. Facilitator can say "let's put this on the discussion list." Addressed at the end of the meeting, so nothing gets lost but flow is protected.
- **Time discipline:** Nothing should run more than 4–5 minutes without tabling it
- **Phase-one thinking:** Don't try to solve everything at once — get a draft one running, collect feedback for a quarter, then iterate to phase two

## Feedback Loop Problem — Org-Wide Pattern

The kids ministry session surfaced a pattern that likely exists across departments:

- Kids leads at locations are supposed to submit weekly reports (already in Teams / CCB)
- They don't do it consistently — Manny has asked at least 10 times in a year and a half
- The system exists; the behavior doesn't
- Root cause: no one at the location level is held accountable for submitting; LPs haven't reinforced it
- Resolution proposed: LPs put weight behind the expectation; it becomes a non-negotiable, not a nice-to-have

**Org implication:** This pattern (systems exist, compliance is inconsistent, accountability is unclear) likely applies to comms intake, ClickUp usage, and any new workflow ECC rolls out. Building in enforcement from the LP level — not just from central — is critical.
