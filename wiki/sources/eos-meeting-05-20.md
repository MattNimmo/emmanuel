## Decisions Made

- ECC will formally adopt EOS (Entrepreneurial Operating System) as its organizational framework, branded "Emmanuel Operating System."
- Andy Ballew will serve as internal EOS consultant (volunteer/advisory role alongside his day job).
- Search for Executive Pastor (XP) is accelerating; budgeting for XP targeted for **2027**.
- EOS vision work (VTO — Vision/Traction Organizer) will begin immediately; target: all EOS components operational within **6 months**.
- Existing meetings and structures will continue unchanged during transition; EOS work is additive.
- An accountability chart (not an org chart) will be developed — one person accountable for each deliverable.
- L10 (Level Ten) meeting structure will be developed for regular leadership rhythms.
- No pause on current work streams; organizational stops are explicitly rejected (Nate cited StratOPS as a cautionary example of pausing = losing 6–9 months).
- Core values will be redefined under EOS (current wall letters do not constitute formal core values; "joy" and "fun" likely to come off the list).
- Performance review process will be tied to EOS values and separated from compensation decisions.
- "Rocket Fuel" (book) ordered in 10 copies; distributed to all present.

## Nate Ruch — Organizational State of the Church

Nate opened with a personal narrative spanning his tenure (2013–2026):

> "I am Both lead pastor and executive pastor right now, and that's not sustainable. And it hasn't been sustainable. And there are things that are suffering as a result."

**Timeline of structural evolution:**
- 2014: Maple Grove added (year 2)
- 2015–16: Capital campaign + above-ground building
- 2017: Elk River approached ECC (not sought); added
- 2021–22: Lakeville added
- Each campus addition = new staffing dynamics, but structure adapted incrementally rather than by design

**The core problem:**
When Executive Pastor Ben left, Nate filled both roles. The DLT (Directional Leadership Team) now all wear "two hats" (ministry area leader + whole-church hat), but no one is functioning as a traditional XP. The result:
- Slow decisions; clunky processes; unclear accountability
- Nate physically unsustainable: "It's taken a toll on my body. It's taken a toll on who I am."
- Nate unable to function as visionary: "I cannot be a visionary right now."
- Staff feel low trust; questions go unanswered; finger-pointing on handoffs

**Succession framing:**
> "I know I will turn fifty-five this summer. I am entering a ten-year window to sixty-five. Just basic common sense says we're going to go through a succession process at somewhere in my sixties."

The restructuring is being framed as building systems now so the church is healthy and succession-ready when the time comes — not because anything is broken beyond repair.

**Nate's self-critique:**
> "I probably have the most changes to make... people don't feel trusted by me... I can get offended by that sometimes."

Named dysfunctions Nate acknowledged:
- Passive-aggressive water-cooler culture: staff talk about each other and about leadership outside the room rather than directly.
- End-around permission-seeking: when a direct report gives direction staff disagree with, some go directly to Nate to get a different answer.
- Staff who are here because they have direct access to Nate — that access will feel different once an XP is in place; this will require a culture shift.
- "The church is not getting the best version of me right now."

## Andy Ballew — EOS Overview

### Why change? (Named problems)

Andy presented a candid assessment of ECC's current state:

**Strengths acknowledged:**
- Sisterhood events (at capacity)
- Weekly multisite live streaming (rare in multisite world — most record Saturday and play back)
- Worship culture + original music reaching globally; volunteer development "best in class"
- Production quality relative to spend
- Next Gen attendance rates vs. Sunday
- Financial responsibility and data discipline

**Named problems:**
- Attendance relatively flat
- High-schoolers and high-functioning volunteers face friction getting anything done (budget approval, room access, systems access — all clunky)
- Bench strength is thin: "if one of you decides to leave, there's not many people behind you"
- "Firefighter culture": remarkable things happen 16 minutes before an event, but through heroic effort and last-minute buying
- Location experiences are not consistent: first impressions, worship quality, kids' ministries all diverge
- Systems are clunky (example: 13-year attendees being asked to re-enter contact info on every form)
- Finger-pointing and confusion on accountability; no clear "who owns this"
- Silos: each team functions as its own mini-system pulling in different directions
- "Culture of honor" can prevent honest feedback from surfacing upward; may suppress truth-telling

JonCarlos Velez:
> "We want an off-road vehicle but continue to drive a sports car."

JonCarlos on unity:
> "There is a lack of strategic unity within our decision makers and our staff."

JonCarlos on family vs. team culture:
> "We function more as a family rather than a team. At a family, you can lock yourself up in the room and still be called family and operate out of this complete dysfunction and still have love. We need to start working less as family and more as team."

## EOS Framework (Andy's Presentation)

EOS (Entrepreneurial Operating System) = structured framework for running an organization consistently. Six core components:

| Component | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Vision** | Core values, core focus/mission, 3-year target, 10-year target; codified in a VTO (Vision/Traction Organizer) |
| **People** | Accountability chart; who's accountable for what; not an org chart — one person per deliverable |
| **Data** | Scorecards, KPIs, metrics the organization actually believes in |
| **Issues** | Structured process for surfacing, discussing, and resolving issues in meetings |
| **Process** | Standardized best practices; documented "how we do things here" |
| **Traction** | Rocks (90-day priorities); L10 meeting cadence |

**VTO (Vision/Traction Organizer):** The primary EOS planning tool. Contains core values, core focus, 3-year picture, 10-year target, target market, and marketing strategy. Cascades down to department-level VTOs.

**L10 (Level Ten) Meetings:** Structured weekly leadership meetings. Goal: attendees rate the meeting 8–10 out of 10. Very organized; data-driven; issues are raised, discussed, and assigned quickly.

**Accountability Chart:** Replaces org chart in EOS thinking. One person — not a committee — is accountable for each function. Handoffs are explicit.

### EOS Adaptation for ECC

- Not adopting EOS wholesale; building an "Emmanuel Operating System" using EOS as a foundation
- Will borrow from EOS, Scaling Up, Lean, OKRs, and prior ECC work (StratOPS) as appropriate
- Core values will be redefined: current wall letters not specific enough; "joy" and "fun" likely to come off
- Mission 52 must be translated to department-level "how" — not yet done

## Executive Pastor (XP) Search

- Budget preparation began in 2026 budget cycle; XP position targeted for **2027** hire
- EOS and XP search running simultaneously (intentional — they must inform each other)
- XP must be an "integrator" type (per *Rocket Fuel* framework): someone who excels at execution, connecting dots, and managing the operating system
- Nate is the "visionary"; the XP will be the integrator
- Looking for someone with EOS-compatible mindset; ministry background preferred but not required
- Andy warned: people who currently have direct access to Nate must shift to working through the XP — this will feel different
- Timeline for XP hire: not stated; search accelerating
