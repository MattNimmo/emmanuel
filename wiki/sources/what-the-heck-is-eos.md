## Key Tools Documented (with diagrams)

### The V/TO (Vision/Traction Organizer)
Two-page master planning document. Left page = Vision (Core Values, Core Focus, 10-Year Target, Marketing Strategy). Right page = Traction (3-Year Picture, 1-Year Plan, Rocks, Issues List). See [[eos-framework]] for the full 8-question breakdown.

### The Accountability Chart
Not an org chart — no dotted lines. Each seat contains:
- Function name
- Person's name
- ~5 bullet-point roles/responsibilities

Key structural concept: **Visionary vs. Integrator** at the top of the chart. The Visionary holds big ideas and relationships; the Integrator holds LMA (Leadership, Management, Accountability) and execution. Having both defined prevents the Visionary's ideas from bypassing the execution layer.

### The People Analyzer
Grid: team members (rows) × Core Values (columns). Rating: **+** / **+/−** / **−**. Plus three GWC columns: **Get It** / **Want It** / **Capacity to Do It** (Y or N). "The Bar" = minimum acceptable standard. Goal: Right Person in Right Seat = meets Core Values bar AND scores Y/Y/Y on GWC.

### The Scorecard
Weekly measurables dashboard. 4 columns: **Who / Measurable / Goal / Date**. Should use *leading* activity-based indicators (what drives results), not *lagging* result metrics. Each person has at least one number they own weekly.

### Rocks
Quarterly priorities broken into 90-day chunks. Must be **SMART** — vague rocks create murky accountability. Concept origin: Covey's *First Things First* rocks/pebbles/sand jar. Reviewed weekly (on/off track only); no discussion unless it becomes an IDS item.

### The Level 10 Meeting (L10)
Full 90-minute weekly agenda — documented precisely. See [[eos-framework]] for complete agenda. The **IDS segment is 60 of 90 minutes** — this is the productive core, not a postscript. Issues are listed then addressed in priority order: Identify → Discuss → Solve.

### The EOS Organizational Checkup
20-question self-assessment, 1–5 scale. Max 100. Covers all 6 components. Useful as a baseline before rollout and to track progress toward 100% strong in all areas.

## Most Relevant Passages for ECC

**On Vision misalignment (p. 13–14):**
> "Before EOS, I would get told one thing by my manager and another thing by the owner of the company. We were running around in circles, making the same mistakes again and again." — Customer service rep at Houston transportation company

This maps directly to ECC's "inspiration as permission" pattern — Nate says something, it gets interpreted as directive, comms gets pulled in conflicting directions.

**On the Visionary/Integrator:**
The chart on p. 74 shows the Visionary above the Integrator, both at the top of the Accountability Chart. The Visionary's roles: Big Ideas, Big Relationships, Culture, Industry Trends. The Integrator's roles: LMA, P&L, Remove Obstacles, Special Projects. Formalizing this at ECC would give Matt/JC structural authority to filter Nate's inspiration.

**On the Level 10 Meeting (p. 93–94):**
Weekly meetings create 52 "spikes of activity" per year vs. 12 for monthly. More frequent = less procrastination. The EKG metaphor: it's the heartbeat of the org. Without it, people delay until just before the meeting.

**On SMART Rocks (p. 85):**
"Read a book" → "Read *What the Heck Is EOS?* and discuss all chapter questions with my manager." The specificity is what makes accountability real.
