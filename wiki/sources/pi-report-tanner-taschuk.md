## What the Report Says

**Behavioral style:** Very conscientious and disciplined. Particularly careful, thorough, and accurate. Exacting; strives to produce results that precisely meet the established goals. Works best where there are **clear standards, expectations, reporting relationships, and measurements for the work.**

**On process:** Stable and dependable; consistent in actions; sufficient patience for repetitive work if needed. Believes in having and following well-defined processes and procedures. **Makes changes only when convinced, with hard evidence, that the new way will be better.** When rolling out changes, will be very tactical — thinking through exactly how it should be done, creating a complete plan, having solutions to pitfalls.

**Motivation:** Unselfish; motivated by a strong sense of duty. Derives satisfaction from doing good work for the company or team. **Values recognition of dedication and specialized skill.** Takes work responsibilities very seriously.

**With people:** Rather unobtrusive and quiet. Friendly and pleasant with people he knows well; reserved and polite in meeting new people. General social behavior governed by a strong sense of propriety — concern for doing and saying the right thing.

## Management Implications (Matt managing Tanner)

Two profiles with significantly overlapping patterns (both Objective, both Moderately Collaborative, both Steady-leaning) — this creates natural harmony but also shared blind spots:
- **Neither will escalate conflict.** Matt's "uncritical listener who won't rock the boat" + Tanner's "may avoid conflict" = problems surface late or not at all. Explicit check-ins on blockers are essential.
- **Tanner needs crystal-clear expectations.** Ambiguity is a genuine productivity killer for him. Don't assume he'll infer intent or ask.
- **He does not self-direct well into new territory.** He needs the first pass defined; then he executes it with quality. This is not a weakness — it's the spec he runs on.
- **Recognition matters.** He values acknowledgment of dedication and specialized skill — not status (like Sara) but competence. A specific "you handled that well" lands differently than a generic compliment.
- Matt and Tanner are likely natural working partners — both process-following, both objective, both reserved. The risk is insufficient creative tension; neither will push the other toward discomfort.
