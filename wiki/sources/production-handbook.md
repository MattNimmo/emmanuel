**TLDR:** Official Emmanuel Production (Tech Team) handbook covering team identity, vision, role hierarchy, and detailed responsibilities for all 13 production roles across Broadcast Producing and Video Directing tracks. Signed by the Service Producer. Primary reference for anyone serving in production at any ECC campus.

---

## Overview

Written on behalf of Pastor Nate and Jodi Ruch. The Tech Team is described as "a bunch of goofy, smart people executing at a high level." The intro letter emphasizes that they are not serving the building or the production leader — they are serving Christ through the Tech Team.

> "We care about you more than what you do; it's about becoming better disciples of Christ."

---

## Who We Are — Identity Values

**Dependable:** "You do what you said you would do. Simple as that!" Deliver under tight timelines and high expectations.

**Approachable:** Production gets intense. At no point does intensity dictate behavior. No projecting "leave me alone, I'm busy, irritated, or worse." Stay approachable to all.

**Flexible:** Details come in late, even last minute. Stay flexible in those moments and strategize afterward about how to improve.

---

## Vision

> "Vibrant, Spirit-filled worship experiences." — Pastor Nate Ruch

The Tech Team's job is to support that vision — making each song or message richly potent and memorable to point people to Christ. Services break away from money-making productions that put on a show; instead, minimize distractions and inspire people toward the Gospel.

**Key principles:**
- The service experience starts in the parking lot — from First Impressions to lobby music to the sanctuary
- **Service length goal: 70–80 minutes**
- "The sermon isn't over until the response has been sung."
- **"Services, not shows."**

## Broadcast Producing Roles

### Service Producer
"Details, details, details." Ultimately responsible for any feedback from leadership or anyone experiencing the service. Influences and leads all aspects of the service.

**5-question checklist before any service:**
1. Do we have the vision of how to pull it off?
2. Do we have the people to pull it off?
3. Do we have the content to pull it off?
4. Do we have the time to pull it off?
5. Do we have the experience to pull it off (i.e., rehearsal)?

### Broadcast Timekeeper
Manages allotted time segments via the time-keeping device.

- Time-keeping device logged in and running before service
- Participate in pre-service meetings and debrief meetings
- Filter: *"If fifty people were relying on my role for their role to be excellent, what's the best way to serve them?"*

### Inter-Location Comms
Bridge between the content-gathering room (broadcast campus) and content-consuming rooms (other locations). Creates time stamps that act as play buttons for locations to start their stream.

- Create time stamps during dress rehearsal and services for location time sync
- Communicate to all locations in the "chat" when and why a time stamp was created
- "Measure twice and cut once" — double-check the scrub
- Proactively communicate when broadcast deviates from the plan

### Stage Manager
Manage all movements of items and gear on/off platform during services.

- Wear a headset to stay in the loop on timing and communication
- Ask Service Producer proactively: "How can I be helpful in pulling off this service?"
- Bring out platform pieces at the correct time, every time
- Be mindful of being on camera. Smile!

### Makeup Artist
"People are about to pour their hearts out on camera." The Pastor's Hub is a relaxation zone — be complementary, professional, with great conversational skills.

- Arrive at Pastor's Hub at scheduled time
- Powder Host Pastor and anyone on the Planning Center speakers list
- When Pastor Nate or the message communicator arrives, powder them ASAP then let them retreat to their quiet room
- Tidy up and leave it better than you found it

### Audio

**Vision:**
- "Pastor Nate came up with the idea of Super Sunday so that we could push the boundaries of what we can do in our Sanctuaries. Not every Sunday is Super Sunday."
- Amplify the platform experience so the back row can hear clearly
- Worship mix: vocals just peeking above the band sonically ("even")
- **Loudness standard: 88–94 dB during full energy moments**

**FOH (Front of House):**
- Deep knowledge of audio consoles and systems
- Understand Dante Virtual Sound Card and system routing
- Monitor and engineer in-ear monitor mixes; troubleshoot personal mixers
- Spectrum analysis software capability
- Collaborate with WL and MD to create FOH mix
- Monitor and maintain safe audio levels for attendees

**BOH (Back of House):**
- Mix the online broadcast for band and spoken word segments
- Dante Virtual Sound Card + WAVES Sound Grid
- Instrument replacement software
- Troubleshoot routing issues during rehearsal or services
- Collaborate with Online Producer and Pastor to ensure exceptional online audio levels
- Monitor online stream audio levels and sync

### Lighting

**Vision:**
- "If words communicate meaning, lighting communicates mood."
- "If the lighting person doesn't turn on the lights, the service doesn't start."
- Create an engaging in-room experience where people can see others in worship
- **"Think sunsets, not lightning bolts."**
- House lighting: bright enough to read a paper Bible during message
- House lighting half as bright during worship vs. message
- **Haze: ever-present but never noticed**
- Location lighting coordinates with broadcast campus during message

**Lighting Designer:**
- Design lighting using colors and intensity to match atmosphere of each service section
- Organize cue lists following the Planning Center service run sheet
- Use macros for house lighting, key lighting, platform elements, hazers, media servers
- Dynamic lighting shifts according to song structure (verse, chorus, bridge); note drum hits, guitar solos, huge vocal parts
- **Lighting programmed and ready no less than one day in advance**

**Lighting Tech:**
- Execute cues programmed by Lighting Designer
- Ensure all platform people fully lit with key lighting at appropriate times
- Monitor haze levels ("like smoking ribs, low and slow")
- Attend dress rehearsal with a sense of urgency — **"dress rehearsal is first service"**
- Be ready to adapt when unannounced changes occur (e.g., extended altar time)

## Video Directing Roles

### Video Directing (Vision)
The Video Director is "the ace in the hole" — they choose what lives online forever. Attention to detail, magnanimous, strong, level-headed, emotionally stable. **This role is Staff-led at Emmanuel.**

Shot philosophy by context:
- **Screen support:** eliminate broadcast distractions; capture clean video
- **Worship — online:** dynamic movement, wider room shots
- **Worship — in-room:** leading vocalists or instrumentalists; lyrics visible and timely
- **Message — for locations:** tighter shots (head-to-toe / head-to-knee); present the pastor to the room
- **Message — online:** wider, more atmospheric; present the room to the viewer

### Live Video Director
Lead and execute the live service run sheet: rolling graphics, rolling videos, changing camera shots, instructing all video team personnel.

- Capture live service for in-room, locations, and online audience
- Production that does NOT distract from the presentation of the Gospel
- Instruct and train production volunteers
- Plan capture approach through rehearsal and pre-service conversations (sometimes days before)
- ALWAYS prepared for the next transition or last-minute change in the live service flow

### Message Notes
Creates all graphic support for the message: scriptures, pictures, graphs. **What this person clicks is seen at ALL locations and lives online forever.**

- Arrive by scheduled time Sunday morning to input notes
- Work with Director and Producer on appropriate graphics for current series
- Double-check spelling and punctuation in every graphic
- Find Service Producer or Communications Lead and review all graphics before concluding
- Expected through all services, whether one, two, or three

### I-Mag Director
For Image Magnification (I-Mag) screens — provides the in-room viewer a front-row visual experience.

- Support Live Video Director; change shots, add graphics, roll videos as instructed
- Direct "message only" using 75% Camera 3 / 25% Camera 2 on a head-to-toe shot
- Roll countdown videos or other in-room specific content

### Graphic Operator
Operate ProPresenter as an extension of the worship team and speaker. Know the lyrics of the songs being performed.

- Create and edit items in ProPresenter
- Video rolls: clear screen → Director says "stand by to roll" → roll on "roll"
- Service countdown clock rolls 5 minutes before service; be in position 10 minutes prior
- Pre-service loop running at least 30 minutes prior to service

### Shading
Adjust iris levels of all cameras to the Director's preference, including black levels and color adjustments.

- Black level balance at start of shift; white balance before every service
- Color-match all cameras; inform Director of abnormal issues
- Prepare record deck: format drives and video cards on arrival
- Stay one step ahead of the director — all cameras technically ready to go live
- If requested, share with Director who is approaching the platform

### Cameras 2&3
Control cameras to convey emotion on platform and bring online/location participants "in the room."

- Move cameras smoothly and without distraction for attendees
- Attend pre-service/run-through meeting; run camera during rehearsal
- Always zoom all the way in and check focus before going live
- **Camera 2 default (message):** head-to-toe shot
- **Camera 3 default (message):** head-to-waist shot

### Cameras 1&4
Same core responsibilities as Cameras 2&3 (convey emotion, smooth movements, rehearsal attendance, zoom/focus check). Specific default shots not defined separately in the handbook.
