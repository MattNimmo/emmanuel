## Key Topics

### 1. Protecting Focused Work Time ("Tenacity Mode")

Dr. Lori framed Matt's core challenge: managing people always wins over focused task work. The "revolving door" of interruptions means checklist work spills into evenings and early mornings.

Historical precedent: Phil and Michelle literally hid in the building to get focused work done.

**Recommendation:** Designate a recurring time block — team knows Matt is in "tenacity mode" and unavailable. Matt mentioned using Tuesdays for Sparrow focus time as a parallel model.

---

### 2. Team Lacks Tenacity Working Genius

- Confirmed: no one on the comms team has Tenacity as a Working Genius (it's a competency for some, including Tanner, but not a genius)
- The team is in a high-output, high-tenacity *role* — this tension is real
- **Kylie** (incoming project manager, joining early May at ~20 hrs) is expected to be the structural "push" for getting things across the finish line
- EOS meeting structure (rocks check-in, issue list) is Dr. Lori's recommendation for structuring meetings to push through to completion
- Reference: *Death by Meeting* (book) also mentioned

---

### 3. Nate's "Inspiration as Permission" Problem

> "People will take inspiration from Pastor Nathan as permission." — Matt Nimmo

When Nate shares an idea, others interpret it as a directive and begin requesting comms support. This creates an endless stream of misaligned, competing priorities.

**Dr. Lori's framing:** Nate is a Visionary. When he speaks vision, it's often future-oriented and exploratory — not a directive for today. Staff need to learn to ask: *"Is this an idea we can take or leave? Is it vision for today or tomorrow?"*

**Recommended action:** Matt and JC need to be explicitly empowered by Nate to confirm green-light stages. Once Nate has approved a direction, Matt/JC own the call that the creative is locked — no new inputs. See [[comms-intake-process]] §"Inspiration is not permission."

---

### 4. Brand Resources & Comms Resource Channel

Past frustration: ministry teams created materials that were rejected by comms because no guidelines existed.

Dr. Lori recommended reviving a **lookbook** with brand resources: fonts, hex codes, color palette, logo usage, photo style guides.

Matt confirmed [[jess-porter]] is already leading the resurrection of the **Comms Resource Channel** — this equips Youth, Kids, Jodi, and other departments to produce brand-aligned materials without routing everything through comms.

---

### 5. Annual Capacity Tiering

Dr. Lori's framework for calendar-based capacity planning:

| Tier | Description | Examples |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Tier 1 — Always-on** | Recurring, non-negotiable, high-production | Christmas, Easter, Mother's Day, Fall Kickoff |
| **Tier 2 — Secondary** | Scheduled but lower production | Ministry events, department support |
| **Pet Projects** | Fulfilling but time-intensive; must be limited + scheduled | Sara's merch (30–60 hrs); ELC garments |

Insight: Sara's merch work is her highest-energy, most fulfilling work — but also her largest single time commitment. Should be limited to 1–2/year, scheduled during June/July lower-load window.

See [[communications-capacity-planning]] for the full seasonal load map.
