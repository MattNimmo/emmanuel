## Core Content Philosophy

### Don't optimize for views — optimize for spiritual impact
Luke's central argument: the algorithm rewards content that keeps people engaged, and spiritual content that *actually* connects does this naturally. If you chase views, you lose. If you chase genuine spiritual moments, the algorithm follows.

> "If you focus on content as what we know — content is not like viral code, but content our content is trench and soul, spirit — then that will be the right direction."

### The listening team model
Sundays are the moment. But Monday through Saturday is where the content work happens. The model:
1. Attend the service
2. Listen with spiritual discernment — journal moments that quicken your spirit
3. Return to the editing room; find and cut those moments
4. Distribute as bite-sized content throughout the week

This applies to both sermon content and worship music.

### Algorithm insight
- Algorithms track "description of experience" — someone who is spiritually hungry will get fed spiritual content just as a fitness-minded person gets fed workout videos
- You don't need a large following to get views; you need content that connects
- The person sitting alone with their phone is the target — not aggregate metrics

## Systems Integration Vision

Luke sees a pipeline where:
- **Content creation** (180 videos, sermon clips, worship moments) feeds the algorithm
- **App** delivers that content directly to members mid-week
- **Small groups** receive cut-up sermon content to continue the weekend's thread
- **Member data** (spiritual journey stage, engagement type) informs what content gets delivered to whom

He mentioned having built this type of system for his own use and for companies. Interest in doing something with ECC — "I need this and this and this, and so I built it for me. But now you get this moment too."
