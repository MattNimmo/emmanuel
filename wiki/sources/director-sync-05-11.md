## Broadcast Camera Feedback — Location Experience

After about a month of new cameras and new shots, location pastors gave structured feedback on what works for their in-room experience.

**What's working:**
- Head-on shots (speaker facing camera) — feel personable, connective
- Slightly off-center shots where speaker's eyes still engage toward camera direction
- Closer tighter shots from floor-level when not too extreme an upward angle

**What isn't working:**
- Hard low upshots ("crouching at the altar, pointing up") — not personable, no connection
- Mid-sentence camera switches to radically different angles — disorienting
- Background changes between shots: switching from screen-background to curtain/wood creates lighting, color, and feel discontinuity — *this is the biggest location complaint*
- Accidental shots (person walking across frame, operator switching without readiness)

**Why background matters so much:** Locations *only* see what's on the screen. When the background behind the speaker shifts from a bright LED graphic to a dark curtain, it looks like a completely different production. Brightness, color, and tone all change simultaneously.

**Long-term fix:** LED walls at each location (~$50–75K/location) would eliminate the background-mismatch problem and make wider shots look "in room."

**Short-term direction:** Simpler shot set. Fewer variables = easier consistency. Front-facing, personable framing as the default.

**On the list:** Speaker-facing red tally light (so speakers know which camera is live). Was functional before, broke with old system. On the upgrade list.

**JC's note:** Multi-site best practices research in progress. Also acknowledging different audience contexts: camera variety works well for online/worship — but location experience has tighter demands.
