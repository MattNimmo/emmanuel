## Draining / Frustrating Work — By Person

### Zac Anderson
- **Lack of creative direction** — "I know it when I see it" rarely actually happens; direction is absent when it matters
- **Absorbed tasks from other roles** — managing things absorbed from departed staff at barely enough capacity to maintain, not enough to own, manage, and grow. Result: poor execution and lower standards. "Nobody is happy."
- **Website updates** — fine but not a priority when other things pile up; also a lack of clarity on the website's philosophy

- **Logos on other organizations' videos** — putting the KB partner logo on videos ECC didn't make. Raised as an integrity question; JC clarified this is intentional (Emmanuel is validating KB partners, not claiming authorship). Resolved in the meeting.
- **Process bypasses** — projects that come back through a higher authority after he's already weighed in. Two specific examples:

  **The Keneally Podcast:**
  > Keneallys messaged Zac asking to record a podcast at the Leader Studio. Zac replied: not ideal for their podcast specifically, suggested an alternative (Zoom, different location, etc.). No reply. Three to four weeks later, Deena messages saying "here's the next podcast Pastor Nate is doing with the Keneallys." It's on their platform, branded as a collab. Zac now can't say no to the project but also doesn't want a sub-par edit going out with the Leader brand on it. Outcome: unclear ownership, unclear editing standard, nobody happy.

  **The JoviConnector Videos:**
  > Ashley asked Zac for early release of the Jovi videos. Zac sent a formal schedule with exact dates. Ashley went silent. Twenty minutes later, Pastor Joey messaged Zac directly asking for the same thing. Zac said no, here's the schedule. This time it held — but the pattern (request → Zac declines → escalate to a pastor) is recurring.

- **The common thread:** Both incidents happened right after Audrey left. Matt: *"We haven't had a filter."*

### Sara Wescott
- **Absorbed work from Neil and Audrey:** app management, website updates, ordering/tracking printable orders — all logistical, none creative, all draining
- **Random non-design requests from Olivia:** decorating rooms, putting a clock on a wall, "géorge a room" — framed as fun side projects but not comms work
- **Consistent unsolicited social media input from leadership:** Jodi and Pastor Nate regularly forward social media strategies and ideas they saw elsewhere. Sara experiences this as "stab, twist" — it highlights the gap without solving it. *"We don't have a social media person or the capacity to operate it well. It's not that we don't know how to do it."*

- **Zach Brauer contractor concern:** Sara worries about oversight burden — Jess (not Jess Porter) already messages him 4x/day to get posts up on ELC/youth accounts. If contracted, that management load falls on Sara.

### Jess Porter
- **Bypassing of process:** framing of non-comms tasks as "a fun little project" or "a brain break" when it's actually additional load
- **Miscommunication of what comms does:** other locations think central comms will make their thank-you cards, help decorate rooms, design for internal events. Jess notes she would never have thought to ask central for these at her prior church — she'd use Canva or Amazon
- **Ordering and tracking printable orders:** end-to-end overhead that doesn't belong in a comms role
- **Emotional disconnect from leadership:** Nobody asks "is this actually helpful to the team?" — it's "here's a thing that could be helpful" without checking whether the team has capacity. Quote: *"There is a team of three that have told us four hundred times that they have a lot on their plate."*

## Social Media — The Structural Problem

Two distinct issues came up:

1. **No dedicated person.** Social media needs a full-time person to do well. The team doesn't have one. Treating it as a distributed responsibility doesn't work.

2. **Leadership input loop.** Jodi and Pastor Nate send social media strategies and accounts they follow, meaning well. Sara hears this as pressure to do something she already knows she can't do at that level. The gap between inspiration and capacity isn't being acknowledged.

**Zach Brauer as contractor:**
- Matt has had a preliminary conversation; Zach indicated he wants a contract document in place — a positive sign for accountability
- JC: if it doesn't work, it's a contract relationship; there's a clear exit
- Sara: reservations about the management overhead, but understands the logic
- See [[zac-anderson]] note: Zach Anderson (internal) ≠ Zach Brauer (potential contractor)
