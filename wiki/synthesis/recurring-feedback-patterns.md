**TLDR:** 18 months of Weekend Planning debriefs, cross-referenced with service times data, attendance numbers, and meeting transcripts, reveal a church that executes the planned thing very well and has almost no recovery system when the plan is wrong. The patterns are not random. They cluster around four structural gaps.

---

## Executive Summary

ECC is a multisite broadcast operation that still plans like a single campus. That one sentence explains most of what follows.

When SLP's message runs 40–49 minutes against a 38-minute plan, every location stalls between services. When a guest speaker sends clear content and the team still loads the wrong video, it's because the verification process was designed for a single-room team that can walk over and check. When screen content doesn't exist for a local-preaching Sunday, it's because content creation is centralized but delivery is distributed. When online has no owner, it's because "online" wasn't built into the staffing model — it was added to the broadcast feed and treated as a byproduct.

The twelve recurring patterns are symptoms. The disease is that the multisite model outgrew the planning and ownership structure built to run it.

---

**Four structural gaps drive everything on this list.**

**1. Ownership ends at SLP.**
The things nobody owns cluster precisely around what happens beyond the SLP broadcast: lobby TVs, pre-roll rotation, PCO timer reliability at locations, screen content for local-preaching weeks, Church Online. These aren't random orphans — they're the parts of the service that exist at the edges of the multisite model. The team owns what regulars at SLP see. Nobody owns what guests at ELK, MG, and LV encounter, or what 600–700 people see online every week.

The Tito pre-roll ran for 5 consecutive weeks. It was caught by Pastor Nate's sister — an online regular — not by the team. That's where the ownership gap lives.

**2. The plan doesn't account for what the plan gets wrong.**
ECC is excellent at executing what's in the plan. The debrief record proves it — strong altar moments, tight focused Sundays, Nathan's offering at SLP praised repeatedly over 18 months. What breaks is anything the plan assumed and got wrong: a message time 2–11 minutes under for standard series, a child dedication sign-up link nobody could find, an offering graphic with no sequence specified. The plan is built with single points of assumption and no recovery path when the assumption is wrong.

The Mother's Day debrief praised "tight execution — 75 minutes with a full response and women's prayer." The service times data shows Jodi Ruch's message ran 5 minutes short at every campus. The team didn't execute tighter. The preacher gave them a buffer, and nobody named it that. The lesson taken is the wrong one.

**3. High-complexity calendar windows are treated as normal execution.**
Super Sunday has run long at 9am for multiple years in a row. The October–November KB/Miracle Offering window now stacks: worship night, KB launch, Miracle Offering (4-week series), child dedication, water baptism, gratitude wall, and the start of Christmas — all between October 4 and December 1. Rob Hoskins (Miracle Offering 2026) is a high-profile external speaker whose handling the director-sync-05-04 explicitly flagged. He arrives in the middle of that window.

These are not surprises. The calendar has been running this annual pattern since at least 2024. The planning process hasn't built a different playbook for them.

**4. External content has two failure modes, not one.**
The most common "didn't work" entry across 18 months is late guest speaker notes. The fix being built (group email, Thursday run-through) addresses a second and different failure: Brad Rosenberg sent complete clarity by Tuesday, one video explicitly marked — and the team still loaded the wrong one. Two distinct failures: content not delivered, and content delivered but not verified. Conflating them produces a protocol that solves one and ignores the other.

---

**What the attendance data adds:**
Mother's Day produces 4 commitments on 4,294 attendance. Water Baptism produces 36 on 4,428. The service design for Mother's Day is built entirely for returning attendees — bouquet moment, prayer over women, special song, child dedication. There's no guest-facing call to action. That's a design choice, and it may be the right one. But Nathan Grams' framing at SLP — acknowledging the range of experiences people bring before celebrating moms — was the highest-praise moment in the debrief precisely because it did the work the rest of the service didn't. Not every campus had it.

The commitment data also validates the baptism-as-CTA thesis Nathan raised in his May 6 meeting. The mechanism works. The testimony pipeline Kylie was assigned (6 testimonies, last week of each month) doesn't align with baptism Sundays. The coordination hasn't happened.

---

**What's resolved:** MG Resi (hardware fixed). Child dedication form (moving to locations). Scripts folder (in progress).

**What's a capital decision, not a process fix:** Camera background inconsistency at locations. LED walls solve it (~$50–75K per location). Everything else on this page is a protocol or an org chart.

**What's a data integrity question before anything else:** LV may be running two public services. The 08:45am slot showed a complete service on Mar 8. Neither the service times framework nor the attendance report was built to detect a second LV service. Confirm with LV campus before drawing conclusions from LKV attendance numbers.

## 1. PCO Timer / Countdown Failures

**First seen:** Dec 2024  
**Frequency:** 10+ times across 18 months; appears in every season  
**Calendar correlation:** None — year-round, not tied to any specific series or event type  
**Status:** Open

**Pattern:** PCO timers not firing at locations. Confidence monitors at remote campuses drop their PCO Live connection. Countdown clocks show the wrong time (e.g., 30 seconds when it's actually 10). Locations have to improvise timing manually.

**Key incidents:**
- Multiple instances across all location campuses throughout 2025
- Mother's Day 2026: countdown showed ~30 sec when it was ~10 sec; caused a miscue on when Pastor Jody should walk to stage
- SLP: Nathan Grams had to improvise an entire child dedication from memory when notes glitched

**Root cause signal:** This isn't a one-off tech failure — it's structural. PCO Live connection reliability at locations has never been solved. The issue predates Josh Patterson's tenure as production lead.

**Recommended action:** Establish a weekly tech check protocol before services that explicitly verifies countdown timers at each location. Assign a named owner at each campus (not "volunteers") for PCO Live readiness.

## 2. Late Guest Speaker Notes

**First seen:** Jan 2025  
**Frequency:** Most common single "didn't work" entry across the entire document  
**Calendar correlation:** Spikes during KB Catalytic weekends (Jan), guest-preacher series (Mark/summer, individual message weeks), and any external speaker engagement  
**Status:** Open — only partially addressed (Thursday standard exists for internal speakers; guests still inconsistent)

**Pattern:** External speakers and guests don't provide notes, outlines, or sermon content by Thursday before the Sunday they preach. Comms can't prep screen lyrics/quotes. Production can't build confidence monitors. The Thursday deadline exists for internal speakers, but guest relationships are not enforced the same way.

**Calendar moments where this hits hardest:**
- **January KB Catalytic Weekend** (Paul Hurkman / Venture) — external speaker, outside ECC system
- **Summer Mark series** (Wayne Northup, young communicators) — rotating roster, varying prep discipline
- **Individual message weeks** (Aug–Sep fall leadup; Phil Johnson; Brad Rosenberg) — single high-stakes Sunday, no routine

**Brad Rosenberg (May 4, 2026):** The wrong video played at the top of the service because content routing wasn't confirmed. Speaker notes hadn't been handed off properly. This triggered the formal guest speaker protocol initiative.

**Recommended action:** Build a [[guest-speaker-protocol]] checklist with hard deadlines: content by Tuesday, notes/outline to production by Thursday, full run-through Friday or Saturday. Assign a named escort/point person for every external speaker.

## 3. Camera Shots — Shaky Rovers and Background Inconsistency

**First seen:** Mar 2025 (shaky rovers); ongoing  
**Calendar correlation:** None — appears year-round, but formalized in feedback Apr–May 2026  
**Status:** Open — formalized in May 11, 2026 director sync; LED wall solution identified but ~$50–75K/location

**Pattern two threads:**

**Thread A — Shaky/unprofessional handheld shots:**  
Rover/handheld camera movement flagged as distracting. Volunteer operators who haven't been trained for broadcast aesthetics. Shot framing inconsistent week to week.

**Thread B — Background inconsistency in multisite broadcast:**  
Speakers at MG, ELK, and LV are shot against their local environments (curtains, lobby walls, stage risers). These backgrounds don't match SLP's broadcast environment. Viewers can tell which sermon segments were recorded locally vs. centrally. This is the #1 feedback item from the May 2026 director sync, affecting audience perception of a unified church.

**Speaker camera confusion:**  
Speakers at locations don't know which camera is "live." Tally light improvements are on the list.

**Resolution path:** LED walls (~$50–75K per location) would create background consistency without physical set changes. Short-term: standardized shot framing guidelines + training for location camera operators, and clear tally light protocol for speakers.

## 4. Lobby TVs Not Working

**First seen:** Jun 2025  
**Frequency:** Multiple independent incidents at MG, ELK, and LV throughout 2025–2026  
**Calendar correlation:** None — but tends to surface during high-attention Sundays when the lobby experience matters more  
**Status:** Open — ownership question unresolved as of May 2026

**Pattern:** Lobby TVs at location campuses go dark, loop incorrectly, or show outdated content. Who is responsible for managing and updating lobby TVs is ambiguous — it surfaces in the debrief notes in Jun 2025, Jan 2026, and May 2026 without resolution.

**The ownership gap:** Central comms pushes content, but who loads it to the location lobby displays? Campus coordinators? Production volunteers? Location pastors? No single person owns this at any location.

**Recommended action:** Assign explicit lobby TV ownership at each campus. Build it into the weekly service prep checklist rather than leaving it to whoever notices.

## 5. Super Sunday 9am Service Runs Long

**First seen:** Feb 2025 (explicitly noted as a multi-year pattern in the notes)  
**Frequency:** Every Super Sunday — confirmed multi-year  
**Calendar correlation:** Always February; always 9am service specifically  
**Status:** Open — no structural fix documented

**Pattern:** The 9am Super Sunday service consistently runs long. This was explicitly called out in the Feb 2025 debrief: *"Overall length of the 9am service. It has happened multiple years in a row."* The cause is predictable: special elements (interview, halftime show concept, giveaway QR codes) layered on top of a standard service structure without adjusting the rest of the service to compensate.

**Calendar context:** Super Sunday is always in February. It's a fixed date, a fixed concept, and a known complexity. The pattern has repeated enough times that it can no longer be treated as a surprise.

**Recommended action:** Establish a Super Sunday planning constraint: total service plan cannot exceed 65 minutes pre-build. Every added element must displace something else. Designate a time-keeper with authority to cut content if the first service runs long.

## 6. Child Dedication — Central Form Management Failures

**First seen:** Feb 2025  
**Frequency:** Feb 2025, Mar 2025, and Mother's Day 2026 (full failure)  
**Calendar correlation:** Child dedications occur ~4x/year (Mar, May, Aug, Oct — varies by campus); centralized form causes confusion each time  
**Status:** Partially resolved — moving to location-level management (Nathan Grams / May 2026)

**Pattern:** The centrally managed sign-up form for child dedications created repeated confusion. Families didn't know where to find the link. Deadlines weren't communicated consistently. Campuses received sign-up windows without enough lead time to prepare. The Mother's Day 2026 instance was the most visible failure: ELK had zero families despite running the standard Sunday; SLP had one family when they expected more.

**Resolution direction:** Nathan Grams and location pastors are moving child dedications to location-level management. The location owns the form, the communication, and the curation. This is confirmed in the director-sync-05-11.

**Next dedication:** October 2026. Ideas for structural improvement:
- Move the dedication moment earlier in service (between songs 2–3, worship still playing)
- Split families across two services if numbers are large
- Add a meaningful outside-the-sanctuary moment (class, prayer, connection) so the platform moment doesn't have to carry everything

## 7. Online / Church Online Ownership Gap

**First seen:** Nov 2025  
**Frequency:** Flagged in Nov 2025, Apr 2026, and implicitly in multiple earlier entries about chat hosts and online audio  
**Calendar correlation:** None — structural gap, not seasonal  
**Status:** Open

**Pattern:** Nobody owns Church Online as a location. Chat host is frequently absent or unscheduled. Online audio issues (flagged repeatedly by Neal at SLP) have no single point of accountability. The May 11 director sync identified that treating online as a location means it needs everything a location needs: a host, an audio owner, a service coordinator.

**Attending viewership:** ~600–700 actual active viewers per service. This is not a small audience.

**Recommended action:** Designate an online pastor or online campus coordinator. Build a weekly service prep checklist for online that mirrors what campus coordinators use. See [[online-broadcast-strategy]] for full context.

## 8. Too Many Elements = Cloudy Messaging

**First seen:** Mar 2025 (implicit); May 2026 (JC's explicit flag)  
**Frequency:** Pattern across multiple seasons; JC raised it explicitly after Mother's Day 2026  
**Calendar correlation:** Highest risk during KB/Miracle Offering season (Oct–Nov), Super Sunday, and when external events stack near a high-demand Sunday  
**Status:** Open — JC raised it as a planning discipline issue, not a one-time fix

**Pattern:** Services with too many elements — too many announcements, too many special moments, too many leaders on platform — produce a cloudy message. The congregation doesn't know what the main thing is. JC called it directly after Mother's Day 2026: *"messaging can get cloudy, staff gets heavy"* when external events stack near high-demand Sundays.

**Calendar risk windows (cross-referenced with master calendar):**
- **Jan KB Catalytic Weekend**: Vision Sunday + KB launch + Catalytic speaker in the same 2-week window
- **Oct–Nov KB/Miracle Offering season**: CG series wind-down + KB series + Miracle Offering + Sisterhood Fall + Halloween/Election = highest complexity period of the year
- **Mother's Day**: child dedication + special song + prayer for women + offering moment + message = 5 focal moments in one service
- **Super Sunday**: interview + giveaway + special elements + standard service = same problem
- **Fall Kickoff**: new CG series + handouts + Fall campaign launch = heavy communication load

**Recommended action:** For Category A and B Sundays, establish a maximum of 2 focal moments outside the message. Every additional element requires removing one. Use the service as a communication medium, not a bulletin board.

## 9. New Songs Without Warm-Up Context

**First seen:** Multiple seasons throughout 2024–2025  
**Frequency:** Moderate — flagged across several different series kickoffs  
**Calendar correlation:** Correlates with series transitions (new series = new worship set); highest at Jan and Sep transitions  
**Status:** Open — no formal warm-up protocol documented

**Pattern:** When a new song is introduced without context or a prior week's warm-up, congregational engagement drops. The audience doesn't know the melody, doesn't know the lyrics, and can't participate. This was flagged repeatedly in debrief notes — particularly after series transitions when worship sets reset.

**Calendar correlation:** January (new series + KB = new worship direction) and September (Fall Kickoff = new series = new set) are the two primary introduction windows.

**Recommended action:** JC's existing worship planning process may already account for this, but the debrief record suggests execution isn't consistent. Establish a protocol: new songs must appear in at least two services before they're treated as primary congregational songs.

## 10. Screen Content Unavailable for Local Sundays

**First seen:** Sep 2025  
**Frequency:** Moderate — flagged in Sep 2025, Feb 2026, and recurs whenever local campuses preach independently  
**Calendar correlation:** Individual message weeks (Palm Sunday, local weeks, Memorial Day); Sep local kickoff; any "local preaching" entry in the master calendar  
**Status:** Open

**Pattern:** When SLP broadcasts centrally, locations receive content automatically. When a location campus has its own preacher (LP preaching locally), they need to source their own screen content — lyrics, lower thirds, announcement slides. Campuses frequently don't know where to find the right files, or the files don't exist in time.

**Calendar moments:** See master calendar entries marked "Local" — Palm Sunday 2026 (Mar 29), Memorial Day weekend (May 24–25), and every individual message week where locations preach independently.

**Scripts folder (May 11, 2026):** The director-sync-05-11 confirmed that Matt is creating a scripts folder in service prep so all teams (LPs, hosting, production, comms) work from the same document. This is a direct response to this pattern.

**Recommended action:** Every local Sunday entry in PCO should have a named content owner by Wednesday of that week. The scripts folder (already in progress) is the right structural fix.

## 11. Stalling Between Services

**First seen:** 2025 (multiple references)  
**Frequency:** Moderate — tied to broadcast timing from SLP  
**Calendar correlation:** Highest risk on weeks with complex elements (Super Sunday, special services, guest speakers)  
**Status:** Open — color system (yellow/green/red) referenced but inconsistently applied

**Pattern:** If the SLP broadcast service runs long, location campuses have to fill time between their first and second service. Volunteers and staff stall. The color system (yellow/green/red signal to locations) exists in concept but isn't applied consistently enough to prevent the problem.

**Recommended action:** Assign a named person at each campus responsible for managing the inter-service gap. Pre-build a 5-minute "fill" plan for Sundays identified as high-risk (Category A/B, guest speaker, Super Sunday).

## 12. Lobby Conversations Inside the Sanctuary

**First seen:** May 2026 (explicit callout in Mother's Day debrief)  
**Frequency:** Flagged as "getting worse, not better" — first explicit debrief entry, but treated as an ongoing trend  
**Calendar correlation:** None — volunteer culture issue  
**Status:** Open — Aaron Crouse flagged at FOH; action needed on volunteer culture

**Pattern:** First impressions volunteers having loud conversations inside the sanctuary during the message. Multiple people can hear it from the front of house. This is a volunteer training and culture issue — team members don't understand that their role extends through the end of the message, not just the greeting moment.

**Recommended action:** Volunteer briefing at every service: the sanctuary is "on" from open doors to post-service dismissal. First impressions team is not off-duty during the message.

### A. The Wrong-Video Incident Revealed Two Separate Failure Modes

The Brad Rosenberg incident (May 4, 2026) is documented under "Late Guest Speaker Notes," but it's a different failure. Brad sent complete clarity by Tuesday — one video explicitly identified. The team still loaded the wrong one.

That means there are two distinct external-content failures:
1. **Content not delivered** — the late notes pattern (no clarity, no deadline enforcement)
2. **Content delivered but not verified** — the team had 5 videos in the system and made a wrong guess at the Thursday content check

The director-sync-05-04 made this explicit: Darren confirmed Brad was crystal clear. Josh loaded what the link gave him without watching the content critically. Nobody caught that a "promo-to-organizations" video was wrong for a congregation context.

**Fix decided:** All guest speaker content routes through the `message` group email (all team eyes on the same package). Thursday reframed as a full service-flow run-through, not a file check. These are two different fixes for two different failures.

That's a design choice. It may be the right one. ### C. "Tight Execution" on Mother's Day May Be Partly Preacher Cadence, Not Production

The May 11 debrief celebrated Mother's Day: "75 minutes total, including full response song, salvation moment, and women's prayer. That's tight execution."

The service times data tells a more specific story: Jodi Ruch's message ran 27:15–33:37 across all campuses vs. 35–38:00 planned — consistently ~5 minutes under plan. That buffer absorbed the child dedication, the special song coordination issues, and the longer mid-service. Production didn't create 5 extra minutes; Jodi gave them.

This matters because if credit for tight services gets attributed to production discipline when it actually came from preacher cadence, the wrong lesson gets reinforced. The right lesson: when Jodi preaches, build her shorter cadence into the plan. It's predictable.

### D. The "Stalling Between Services" Root Cause Is Measurable

The stalling pattern (Pattern 11) is attributed vaguely to SLP broadcast running long. The service times data identifies the specific driver: message length.

On standard sermon-series weeks, messages run 40–49 minutes against a 38:00 plan — a 2 to 11-minute overrun that flows to every location simultaneously. On Q&A-format weeks (What About?), messages run near plan. The color system (yellow/green/red) tries to manage this downstream, but the source is upstream: the planned message time is wrong for standard series.

Until 38:00 is corrected to ~44–45:00 for standard series, every location will have unpredictable inter-service gaps every non-Q&A week.
