## Questions

### Q1 — What determines "important" vs. "nice"?

Within P1, not all requests are equal. When two legitimate requests compete for the same week, what's the deciding factor?

- Is it **audience size** — how many people will see or be affected by it?
- Is it **who's asking** — does a request from a campus pastor carry more weight than one from a ministry leader?
- Is it **external vs. internal reach** — does content aimed at people outside the church always win over content for existing members?
- Or is it something else entirely?

**Answer (2026-04-21):** The tiebreaker within P1 is: audience size, growth-orientation, and internal vs. external reach — in that spirit. Sunday-related and externally focused items win. An external-reach item beating an internal one is a standing rule, not a case-by-case call.

*Why this matters:* Without a tiebreaker, the comms lead is making judgment calls on behalf of leadership. This question moves that decision to the right level.

---

### Q2 — Does growth-driving work always outrank member-serving work?

The current framework assumes yes — church growth takes priority over serving existing members when capacity forces a choice.

- Do you agree with that as a standing rule?
- Are there categories of member-serving work that should always be protected regardless — for example, pastoral care content, Growth Track, or Baptism support?
- Is there a scale threshold where a member-serving request becomes high enough priority to compete with a growth-driving one?

**Answer (2026-04-21):** Yes — growth-driving work outranks member-serving as a standing rule. Two protected exceptions regardless of capacity: **Baptism** and **Growth Track**. No scale threshold exists where a member-serving request competes with a growth-driving one.

*Why this matters:* This shapes whether P2 ever gets elevated and which P2 items are worth outsourcing vs. simply skipping.

---

### Q3 — Within P1, what's the tiebreaker when two events collide?

Sisterhood, Worship Night, 21 Days of Prayer, and Kids Camp are all P1. In November 2026, Sisterhood Fall overlaps with Miracle Offering week and KB Series. If the team can only do two of three things at full production quality — what wins?

- Is there a fixed ranking within P1, or does it depend on the year and context?
- Is Sisterhood always protected at the top of P1?
- Should the comms lead have authority to make this call, or does it come back to you?

**Answer (2026-04-21):** No fixed ranking — P1 conflicts are resolved by context. Sunday-related, growth-oriented, and externally focused items win. Sisterhood is not permanently protected at the top; it can move down when needed. Decision authority: **Matt and Joncarlos** make comms team priority calls — does not need to escalate to Nate.

*Why this matters:* November is the highest-risk month in the calendar. Having a pre-decided answer avoids a crisis conversation in the middle of crunch.

---

### Q4 — How should the team handle requests that arrive without enough lead time?

A well-resourced request with proper lead time and a last-minute request for the same deliverable are not the same ask. 

- Should late requests be automatically downgraded in priority, even if the event itself is P1?
- Is there a lead time policy you'd want communicated to ministry leaders — e.g., "graphics requests need 3 weeks minimum"?
- Who enforces that policy when a pastor makes a late ask?

**Answer (2026-04-21):** Matt and JC decide whether a late request stays on the team's plate or gets outsourced. No automatic downgrade rule — it's a case-by-case judgment call at their level.

*Why this matters:* Lead time is one of the biggest capacity drains. A policy here protects the team and sets clear expectations church-wide.

---

### Q5 — Strategic ministry events in P1 — how do we rank them relative to each other long-term?

Worship Night, 21 Days of Prayer & Fasting, and Kids Camp are all in P1 because they're public-facing and strategic. But they vary in scale and visibility.

- Is there a ministry event below Sisterhood but above "do when capacity allows" that you'd always protect?
- Are any of these events candidates for scaling back production in a heavy season rather than outsourcing?

**Answer (2026-04-21):** No fixed middle tier at this time. Yes — in heavy seasons, P1 events can have **scaled-back production** rather than outsourcing. This is approached situationally as needed, not by a preset rule.

*Why this matters:* Helps define the ceiling and floor within P1 without needing to escalate every year.
