**TLDR:** EOS (Entrepreneurial Operating System) is an organizational operating model from the book *Traction* (Gino Wickman) that [[nate-ruch]] is adopting at ECC. It brings clarity to who owns what, aligns departments through shared quarterly goals ("rocks"), and introduces structured meeting rhythms. Expected to roll into ECC culture in 3–6 months from April 2026; currently being piloted in DLT meetings.

---

## The 6 Key Components (The EOS Model)

EOS is built around a wheel — six components that a healthy organization must be 100% strong in:

| Component | Core Question |
|-----------|--------------|
| **Vision** | Is everyone aligned on where we're going and how we'll get there? |
| **People** | Do we have the right people in the right seats? |
| **Data** | Are we managing with measurables, not gut feelings? |
| **Issues** | Do we surface and solve problems quickly and permanently? |
| **Process** | Are our core processes documented and followed by all? |
| **Traction** | Do we have discipline and accountability to execute the vision? |

**The arrows metaphor (as used at ECC):** Without shared goals, people point in different directions — nobody's doing anything wrong, but energy is wasted. EOS aligns the arrows.

## The V/TO — Vision/Traction Organizer

The V/TO is EOS's master planning document. It answers 8 questions in cascading time horizons:

| V/TO Section | Time Horizon | What It Answers |
|---|---|---|
| **Core Values** | Timeless | Who you are; the behaviors expected from everyone; culture definition |
| **Core Focus** | Timeless | What you are (Purpose/Cause/Passion + your Niche — what you do best) |
| **10-Year Target** | Long-range | Where you are going; the "guiding light" big goal |
| **Marketing Strategy** | Ongoing | Target Market/"The List"; 3 Uniques; Proven Process; Guarantee |
| **3-Year Picture** | 3 years out | What the org looks like: Revenue, Profit, Measurables, "What does it look like?" |
| **1-Year Plan** | Next 12 months | The 3–7 most important goals for this year |
| **Rocks** | Next 90 days | The 3–7 most important priorities this quarter, by person |
| **Issues List** | Ongoing | What will stop you or get you there faster |

**ECC application:** ECC doesn't have a documented V/TO yet. EOS rollout will require leadership to answer all 8 questions formally. The Quarterly Rocks (church-wide → department → individual) are the most immediately relevant tool for comms.

## Quarterly Rocks

Rocks = the 3–7 most important things each person must complete in the next 90 days. They cascade:

1. **Church-wide annual goal** (e.g., Mission 52)
2. **Church-wide quarterly rock** (e.g., "What are we doing in Q2 toward Mission 52?")
3. **Department/campus quarterly rock** (e.g., "How does comms align with this?")
4. **Individual rocks** — each team member has their own

**Rocks must be SMART.** Vague rocks = no accountability. Examples:

| Not SMART | SMART |
|-----------|-------|
| "Social media strategy" | "Publish 3-platform content calendar for Q3 by May 31" |
| "ClickUp setup" | "Complete intake form + capacity view build by June 15" |
| "Read EOS book" | "Read *Traction* and discuss 3 key applications with JC by May 30" |

**The rocks/pebbles/sand jar analogy (Covey, *First Things First*):** If you put sand and pebbles in first, the rocks don't fit. Put the rocks in first — the big priorities — and everything else fills in around them.

Rocks are reviewed every week in the Level 10 Meeting (on/off track, nothing more unless it's an issue). At quarter-end, a new set of rocks is set for the next 90 days.

## The Level 10 Meeting (Weekly)

EOS's core meeting structure — 90 minutes, same day/time every week, fixed agenda. Named "Level 10" because a well-run meeting rates a 10/10. **This is what Nate is piloting in DLT.**

**The Agenda:**

| Segment | Time | What Happens |
|---------|------|--------------|
| **Segue** | 5 min | Each person shares one good news (personal or professional); gets people present |
| **Scorecard** | 5 min | Review weekly measurables — on track or not; off-track items go to IDS |
| **Rock Review** | 5 min | Each rock: on track or off track. *No discussion* — off-track rocks go to IDS |
| **Customer/Employee Headlines** | 5 min | Good or bad news about customers or staff; items go to IDS if needed |
| **To-Do List** | 5 min | Review last week's to-dos (7-day action items) — done or not done |
| **IDS** | **60 min** | **Identify, Discuss, Solve** — the heart of the meeting |
| **Conclude** | 5 min | Rate the meeting 1–10; review to-do list; confirm who's doing what |
| **Total** | **90 min** | |

**IDS = Identify, Discuss, Solve.** Issues are listed, prioritized (most important first), then solved one at a time:
- **Identify:** Name the *real* issue — often not what's initially stated
- **Discuss:** Open conversation; everyone speaks
- **Solve:** Make a decision; assign a to-do or rock; issue is resolved permanently (not circled back to next week)

**Critical rules:**
- Issues raised during Scorecard/Rock Review are *listed*, not discussed — they wait for the IDS segment
- If you run out of time on IDS, remaining issues roll to next week's list
- A timekeeper is needed (Dr. Lori flagged this for the DLT pilot)
- No meeting derailment — the structure is the discipline

## The Accountability Chart

Different from a traditional org chart in two key ways:
1. **No dotted lines** — dotted lines mean multiple bosses, which creates confusion. Every seat reports to one person.
2. **Each seat has ~5 defined roles** — not just a job title. Everyone knows exactly what they're responsible for.

**The Visionary / Integrator distinction** — the most important structural insight for ECC:

| Role | Characteristics | ECC Analog |
|------|----------------|------------|
| **Visionary** | Big ideas, big relationships, culture, industry trends, inspiration | [[nate-ruch]] |
| **Integrator** | LMA (Leadership, Management, Accountability), P&L ownership, removes obstacles, executes | Matt Nimmo + JC? |

The Visionary generates energy and direction. The Integrator makes it happen. When both roles are clear, the Visionary can ideate freely *without* unintentionally creating work for the team — because the Integrator owns the filter. This is directly relevant to the "inspiration as permission" problem at ECC: Nate is the Visionary; Matt/JC are the de facto Integrators, but that handoff has not been formalized.

*LMA = Leadership, Management, Accountability — the core job of the Integrator seat.*

## GWC — Get It, Want It, Capacity to Do It

The "right seat" test. For each person in each seat, ask three questions:

| Question | What it means |
|----------|--------------|
| **Get It** | Do they understand what the role requires intuitively? |
| **Want It** | Do they genuinely want to do this work? |
| **Capacity to Do It** | Do they have the time, skills, and bandwidth? |

All three must be **Yes** to be in the right seat. A "No" on any one of them is a problem — even if the person is the right *person* for the company.

**Wrong seat ≠ wrong person.** Being in the wrong seat just means the role doesn't fit. The right seat for them may exist elsewhere in the org.

## The People Analyzer

A tool that pulls together Core Values and the Accountability Chart to assess whether you have the Right People in the Right Seats.

**How it works:**

1. List team members in rows
2. List Core Values as column headers
3. Rate each person per Core Value: **+** (lives it), **+/−** (inconsistent), **−** (doesn't live it)
4. Add GWC columns: **Get It (Y/N)**, **Want It (Y/N)**, **Capacity to Do It (Y/N)**
5. Compare to "The Bar" — the minimum standard for the organization

**Right Person:** Mostly + on Core Values (a few +/− is okay; no −)
**Right Seat:** GWC = Y, Y, Y
**The Bar:** Must meet minimum Core Values threshold AND have all 3 GWC answers as Yes

## The Scorecard

A weekly measurables dashboard — not financial reports, but **leading activity indicators** that tell you if you're on track *before* the results are in.

**Structure:**

| Who | Measurable | Goal | Date |
|-----|-----------|------|------|
| Person name | Specific metric | Target number | Weekly date |

**Examples of good scorecards:**
- Sales: # New Leads, Total Opportunities in $, # Scheduled Appointments, Win Rate %
- Operations: % Utilization, % Service Level, $ Cost per Unit, # Overtime Hours

**ECC comms scorecard analog:**
- Tanner: # sermon clips posted, # Sundays uploaded on schedule
- Sara: # requests completed on time, # social posts published
- Zac: # weekly emails sent, # issues flagged at intake

The scorecard is reviewed in the first 5 minutes of the Level 10 Meeting. Off-track numbers go directly to the IDS list.

## The EOS Implementation Process

The formal rollout sequence (for reference, since ECC is in early stages):

```
90-Min Meeting → Focus Day → Vision Building Day 1 → (30 days) → Vision Building Day 2
                                                                        ↓
                                              Quarterly review every 90 days ("90-Day World")
                                              2-Day Annual Planning each year
```

**Focus Day** produces: Accountability Chart, Meeting Pulse, Scorecard
**Vision Building** produces: V/TO (Core Values through Issues List)
**Quarterly** produces: New Rocks, Issues resolution, Scorecard adjustments

## The EOS Organizational Checkup

A 20-question self-assessment (1–5 scale) to measure current EOS health. Covers all 6 components. Questions include:
- Clear vision in writing, communicated to everyone
- Core Values used for hiring, reviewing, rewarding, firing
- Accountability Chart clear, complete, updated
- Everyone in right seat (GWC)
- Everyone has Rocks (1–7 per quarter)
- Level 10 Meeting in place (weekly, same day/time)
- Scorecard with weekly measurables
- Issues identified, discussed, and solved

Max score = 100. ECC has not yet run this assessment formally.

## Why Nate Wants This

> "It's common in matrix multi-site scenarios to have issues related to who owns what. The idea is that it's just very clear, and you can run in the lanes you have." — Nate Ruch

He also wants to reduce the number of times he's called in to make decisions that should already be delegated. EOS provides the clarity that makes delegation stick — and that formally designates Visionary vs. Integrator roles, so Nate can generate ideas without every idea becoming a directive.
