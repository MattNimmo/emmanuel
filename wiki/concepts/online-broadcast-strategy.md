Nate treats it as a location-level priority; most of the rest of the team doesn't share that urgency. The core unresolved question is org structure: who owns Sunday morning online operations, and is there an online campus pastor?

## Viewership — The Real Numbers

Previous reporting significantly undercounted actual online attendance:

| Platform | Previously Reported | Actual Estimate |
|----------|-------------------|-----------------|
| YouTube | ~112 | ~357 (YouTube counts families/households, not raw views; one TV = multiple devices on same Wi-Fi) |
| Church Online (Pando) | ~50–100 | ~200–300+ |
| Facebook Live | Not tracked | TBD |
| **Total** | **~160–200** | **~600–700** |

- Caleb's reported numbers were the raw YouTube view count — not the household-adjusted count
- Tanner and Matt discovered this discrepancy by investigating how YouTube actually counts viewership
- **Implication:** ECC's online campus is already the size of a mid-sized physical campus and is not being stewarded as such

## What Broke Down — History

- **Jack** was the online campus pastor; when he left, the role was never backfilled
- After Jack: Jodi became the sole person burdened for online; various organizational changes followed
- **Rotation attempt:** ~a year ago, a rotation of 4 pastors was set up to cover online during Sunday services. Failed because:
  - Pastors didn't care enough to stay engaged
  - Rotation was too erratic; volunteers couldn't reach the right person
  - No one had everyone's phone numbers
- **Current state:** Hot potato. Production switcher monitors platforms but nobody owns the pastoral/engagement layer

## Immediate Fix (Proposed)

Matt's proposal — two separate problems to solve:

**Problem 1: Technical uptime**
- Online switcher has all three platforms (YouTube, Church Online, Facebook) visible
- That person confirms live status at 9:02am every Sunday
- If something's down, they know before anyone else and can act
- This is a production/comms responsibility — manageable now

**Problem 2: Online pastoral presence**
- Who hosts the chat? Who prays for online viewers? Who's the face for people watching?
- This is *not* a comms or production question — it's a pastoral one
- Nate's direction: pull [[jodi-ruch]] into the conversation; she's an implementer, already burdened, thinks in systems ("she'll ask: what do we do with the information? Who's making follow-up calls?")
- Longer-term: online pastor role — someone younger, tech-savvy, who can galvanize a volunteer team around the online experience (JC's description: "almost like Jack but more technologically savvy")

## Treating Online as a Location

JC's framework: *"If we treat it as a location, it's a top priority. Right now, nobody's been talking about it that way."*

If online = a location, it implies:
- Sunday morning tech check = non-negotiable (same as a campus doing a sound check)
- Someone is responsible for the "service experience" for online viewers
- Metrics are tracked and reported the same way physical campus attendance is
- Volunteer teams are recruited and developed for the online campus
- Plan a visit / next steps pathway exists for online-to-in-person conversion

**One Church Columbus** had an online campus pastor running their Sunday hosting team. Their social media room (10+ volunteers) fed content to the online experience in real time. The two are connected.
