**TLDR:** ECC's social media is currently functioning as a bulletin board with no dedicated owner and no real strategy. Leadership (Nate, JC, Matt) all agree this is a gap. The vision is to transform it into a neighborhood conversation and organic reach engine — modeled on One Church Columbus's volunteer-powered social media room. The structural blocker is capacity: social media done well needs a dedicated person or team.

---

## Current State Problem

- **No dedicated owner** — social has been a distributed responsibility since the dedicated social media role was lost (Nate: "We had a page position for social media for years, and then somebody said 'we can do this over here' and it got lost")
- **Bulletin board mode** — posts announce events; no community-building, no storytelling, no strategy
- **Low ROI on effort** — 25 likes on a Dr. Nate Ruch audiobook post; feels like a waste of [[sara-wescott]]'s time
- **Leadership input loop** — Nate and Jodi regularly send social accounts and strategies they've seen elsewhere; well-intentioned but experienced by the team as pressure without capacity
- **Cable television drain** — ECC had a dedicated FTE on cable TV for years; "what's the actual result of that? Meanwhile our social media strategy is nonexistent"

## The Vision

### What Social Media Should Be

Nate's framing:
- **A billboard** — awareness; people know ECC exists and what it stands for
- **A neighborhood conversation** — consistent, reliable, relatable; people feel connected between Sundays
- **A purple cow** (Seth Godin) — you don't have to be perfect, just distinctive and consistent; showing up reliably is the strategy

JC's framing:
- Social media should make people feel like they're *there* — driving FOMO that converts to attendance
- It should surface 180-style stories, behind-the-scenes moments, what God is doing across all locations
- It should feel like the actual ECC culture — community, testimony, biblical — not just the Sunday formula

### The One Church Columbus Model

Nate visited One Church Columbus (near Ohio State University) and was deeply impressed by their social media operation:

- A dedicated room — cameras, laptops, multiple monitors, editing stations
- **10+ volunteers** working on Sunday mornings, posting to multiple channels in real time while the service happens
- Channels covered: church main, music, youth, kids, pastor's personal
- A young volunteer runs the whole room — energized, using their gifts
- One staff person's job: follow the senior pastor from the hub through the halls, capture candid moments, post clips later
- **Game plan board** on the wall — Sunday game plan for each channel mapped out in advance

JC on Sara: "If she were to do that, she just wants a space. Sara's very atmosphere. Give her a space, and she'd be pumped."

**Next step:** Send Sara to One Church on a Sunday in June/July to observe and learn.

### Additional Reference Points

- **Blue Church** (Miami) — consistent spirit; handles communications and younger demographics well
- **Bloom Conference** (Miami, 10th–14th of a summer month) — Matt and JC could attend

## Structural Requirements to Do This Well

1. **A dedicated person or volunteer team leader** — someone who can galvanize volunteers, hold the room, manage the game plan. JC's description: "younger face, someone that can help galvanize people; you gotta be people on the YouTube"
2. **A physical space** — a corner in the shoot studio with tables, laptops, devices from IT; atmosphere matters for creative output
3. **A social strategy per platform** — each platform has a purpose; who's it for, what's the CTA, what's the frequency? This does not exist yet
4. **Multi-platform redistribution** — ECC content lives on many platforms (Instagram, Facebook, YouTube, Pando, email, app) with no clear ownership map of who posts what where and why
5. **Volunteer development** — [[jess-porter]] already demonstrated: even a small investment in location volunteers (photographers, social leads) yields outsized returns

## Connection to 180 Videos

The two strategies are complementary:
- [[180-testimony-videos]] are the *content* — the most shareable, culture-reflecting stories
- Social media is the *distribution channel* — the quick-tier 180 is ideal for Reels; the mini-doc is YouTube/Sunday

## Luke Fredrickson — Spiritual Content Pipeline (May 2026)

External tech/content strategist Luke Fredrickson met with Matt, JC, and Nate on May 4 with a complementary framework for content strategy. Key additions to this page:

**Don't optimize for views — optimize for Spirit.** Algorithms track "description of experience." A person who is spiritually hungry will get fed spiritually-oriented content the same way a fitness-minded person gets fed workout videos. Focus on genuine spiritual moments, and distribution follows.

**The listening team model:** Sundays are the moment. Mon–Sat is when the work happens. The model: attend the service → listen with spiritual discernment → journal the moments that quicken your spirit → return to the editing room, find and cut those moments → distribute as bite-sized content throughout the week.

**Systems integration vision:** This isn't just social content — it's a full pipeline: Sunday moment → social cut → app mid-week delivery → small group content (cut-up sermon). Member data (spiritual journey stage, engagement type) informs what content gets delivered to whom.

**Status:** Exploratory. No formal engagement. JC and Matt both interested; Nate in the room. See [[luke-fredrickson]] and [[luke-fredrickson-05-04]].

**JC's direction (May 4 creative meeting):** Sara should build a social media volunteer team. Actively recruit this summer. Think of 3 volunteer shoulder-taps. Think about a dedicated creation space (A-List Services space was mentioned). "This is our guinea-pig season."
