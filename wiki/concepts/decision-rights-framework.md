**TLDR:** A 1–5 scale defining who owns a given decision — Location campus or Central org. Created by [[nathan-grams]] (SLP Campus Pastor). Not currently in active use at ECC, but the terminology and concept are familiar across leadership. Can overlay the [[comms-priority-framework]] to clarify *who decides* on top of *what gets done*.

---

## The Scale

| Level | Label | Meaning |
|-------|-------|---------|
| **1** | Location Decision with Churchwide Mindfulness | Campus owns it; just stay aware of the broader church |
| **2** | Location Decision with Central Input | Campus owns it; Central gets a voice before the call is made |
| **3** | *Requires Clarifying Ownership Conversation* | Gray zone — ownership is genuinely unclear and must be negotiated |
| **4** | Central Decision with Location Input | Central owns it; Location gets a voice before the call is made |
| **5** | Central Decision with Location Awareness | Central owns it; Locations are informed but not consulted |

The scale is a spectrum: 1–2 = campus-led, 4–5 = centrally-led, 3 = contested/unclear → resolve with a direct conversation.

---

## How to Use It

Assign a number to any decision domain (e.g., "what series runs on a given weekend" = 5; "which volunteer leads usher team" = 1). The number isn't a rule — it's a shared starting point that surfaces who needs to be in the conversation and who gets the final call.

**Level 3 is diagnostic, not a resting state.** If a decision lands at 3, the action is to have the ownership clarification conversation, not to leave it there.

---

## Relationship to the Priority Framework

The [[comms-priority-framework]] answers: *What gets done?* (P0–P3 tiers by audience and growth impact)

The Decision Rights Framework answers: *Who decides?*

They can overlay: a P0 comms item (Sunday series) is almost certainly a Level 5 Central decision on direction, while a P2 campus-specific event might be a Level 1 or 2. Mapping priority tier against decision level could help clarify when Location needs Central sign-off vs. when they can just execute.

---

## Status

Not currently in active use at ECC. The framework is a familiar concept within leadership — terminology is known but it has not been formally adopted or deployed to map specific decisions.
